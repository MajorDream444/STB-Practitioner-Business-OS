# Human Context — Facilitator Protocol

> **DRAFT — INACTIVE.** Not approved for use. See [the index](README.md) and
> D-035. This protocol should be reviewed by a qualified mental-health
> professional before activation.

## Role

A Human Context conversation is **led by a human** (D-027). The facilitator
listens, asks open and skippable questions, reflects back the practitioner's own
words, and records only what the practitioner approves. The facilitator is **not a
therapist**, does not interpret, and does not treat the practitioner as a
patient. The practitioner is the authority on their own experience.

## Who (provisional, D-032)

The founder, after the preparation below, as a pilot arrangement. The aim is a
repeatable protocol, not dependence on one person's intuition. Future HAMAL
facilitators complete the same preparation and a supervised practice review.

## Preparation (all topics, recorded)

1. Informed consent, including how to ask, how to hear "no", and withdrawal.
2. Supportive listening: reflecting without interpreting.
3. Boundaries: what a business facilitator does not do.
4. Recognising and responding to distress, using this protocol's escalation steps.
5. Confidentiality and data handling (access, storage, deletion protocols).
6. Referral procedures and the directory
   ([REFERRALS-AND-ESCALATION.md](REFERRALS-AND-ESCALATION.md)).

Mental-health first-aid or relevant trauma-informed facilitation training is
**useful but does not qualify anyone to provide therapy** and must not be
described as if it did. Which courses count is for the owner to decide.

## Conduct

**Do**
- Check consent and pace at the start of each conversation.
- Offer choices: pause, continue with business work, or stop for today.
- Use the practitioner's words, verbatim, as theirs.
- Read back what will be recorded; record only what is approved.

**Do not**
- Diagnose, label, or suggest causes for how someone feels or acts.
- Infer trauma, conditions, motives or history from business behaviour.
- Probe emotional material, or ask for history, to "complete" a diagnostic.
- Treat a reflective framework as evidence about the person.
- Let anything said here change a severity score, a route, or a price (D-026).
- Facilitate when unwell, rushed, or in a conflict of interest with the
  practitioner. Decline or reschedule.

## Session structure

**Before**
1. Confirm consent is current for this conversation.
2. Confirm where the practitioner is physically located and a way to reach them
   if the call drops. (Needed for any safety response.)
3. Agree a stop signal and that skipping any question is fine.

**During**
4. Open, skippable questions of the example style in [CONSENT.md](CONSENT.md).
5. If strong emotion arises: acknowledge it plainly, offer a choice, do not
   probe, follow their lead; offer referral information if they want to work on it
   (summary of V1.2 steps; full procedure in the escalation protocol).

**After**
6. Read back the practical accommodations proposed. The practitioner edits,
   approves, or removes any of them.
7. Record **only approved** accommodations. No verbatim session notes beyond
   what the practitioner approves.
8. Note, with no content: that the conversation took place and its length.

## Software assistants

May do administrative work and draft accommodations from approved structured
fields **for human review**. May not facilitate, interpret, advise on emotional
matters, retain content, or handle any safety situation (D-027).

## Reviews

The protocol is reviewed before activation and then on a schedule set by the owner,
and after any incident. Findings are recorded without personal content.
